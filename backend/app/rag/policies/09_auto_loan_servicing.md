## Payment processing and fees
Auto loan servicers should credit payments according to the contract as of the date they are received, including payments made through online or mobile channels. CFPB supervisory reports have identified unfair practices such as misapplying payments, charging late fees on payments made on time, and assessing fees not authorized by the contract.

## Repossession
Repossession is governed mainly by state law and Article 9 of the Uniform Commercial Code, which allows a secured lender to take possession after default only if it does so without a breach of the peace. CFPB supervisors have found it unfair to repossess vehicles after a borrower made a payment or entered an agreement that should have prevented repossession, or to withhold personal property found in a repossessed vehicle.

## Title, lien release and co-borrower changes
Releasing a lien after payoff and processing title changes are governed by state law and the loan contract, which often set deadlines. Delays or errors in releasing a lien or updating a title can prevent a consumer from selling, refinancing or registering the vehicle and should be handled promptly with clear status updates. Removing a co-borrower usually requires refinancing or the servicer's approval under its credit policy.

## Add-on product refunds
When a loan is paid off early or a vehicle is a total loss, consumers may be owed refunds of unearned premiums for add-on products such as GAP coverage or service contracts. CFPB supervisors have cited servicers for failing to ensure these refunds are paid.
